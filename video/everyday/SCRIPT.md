# Meet the Household: one week with Home SOC

*A family guesses they own seven gadgets, finds out it is eighteen (one of them a camera nobody remembers buying), and over one ordinary week a calm new housemate called Home SOC helps them find out what is on the Wi-Fi, what matters, and how to fix it, one day and one fix at a time.*

Final script for the everyday-audience walkthrough of Home SOC. Written 2026-09-22 for the "Stone & Sage" dashboard with plain-language labels. Estimated runtime: **9.9 minutes** at 150 words a minute, including [beat] (0.6 s) and [beat 1.2] (1.2 s) pauses.

Spine: "the-household" draft (preferred by two of three judges), with the judges' grafts from "friendly-host" and every flagged accuracy problem fixed. Running gags: the printer (01, 03, 11, 14), the camera and the drawer (01, 05, 06, 12, 14), the umbrella (07 to 08).

## Voice notes

- Overall: a warm, amused neighbour who has been through this with their own gadgets. Never a salesperson, never a lecturer. The viewer is clever and busy; the gadgets are the silly ones.
- Pace: about 150 words a minute, a little slower for any line that defines a term ('called ports', 'called an IP address', 'That's DNS', 'called KEV', the 94% sentence). Let each [beat] breathe; a [beat 1.2] is a full stop plus a small smile.
- Cold open (01): conversational and quick, as if asking a friend across the table. 'The printer counts.' is flat and matter-of-fact; 'would like it noted [beat] that it has always counted' is said with the printer's wounded dignity, not a silly voice. Reach that line within about twelve seconds.
- Delighted (let the smile show): the printer lines, 'It's a school night', 'aged considerably worse', 'exactly as careful as it sounds', 'Just think of him as Kev', 'Fair: they've never met', 'Nobody complains', the final printer line.
- Reassuring (lower, slower, warmer): 'Don't panic. It's low for two fixable reasons.', 'It isn't evil. Just very old on the inside.', the 94% explanation, 'A filter, not a force field', 'It won't make you hacker-proof. Nothing will. It turns a vague unease into a short to-do list.'
- Slow down: the doors explanation in 05 (each clause is a new idea), the KEV distinction in 07 (pause between the recall notice and the news bulletin), the two DNS limits in 10, and the honesty lines in 11.
- The severity words (03) are read as five calm instructions with a small pause between each, not as a list: 'Fix now. Fix this week. Worth fixing. When you have time. Good to know.'
- Say 'Kev' like a person's name. Spell out 'P D F dot E X E' letter by letter, deadpan. Say the IP as 'one forty-two', never the full address.
- Never sound alarmed. Words like 'attackers' and 'burglars' are said evenly, as information. The film should feel like a to-do list, not a warning.
- Close (14): slow the last 20 seconds right down. 'Seventeen.' lands flat and satisfied; 'The camera's in the drawer.' is a small shrug; hold the [beat 1.2] before the printer line and deliver it exactly like the cold open, so the audience hears the echo.
